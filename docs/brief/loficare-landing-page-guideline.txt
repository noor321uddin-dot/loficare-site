Lofi Care landing page: design and content
guideline for the creative team
Status: creative brief for delegation. Positioning: Lofi Care is a complete hospital and diagnostic
centre management platform for Bangladesh, launched as one brand across three audiences
on a single page. The online appointment system is the one module live and free to start
today; the full platform opens through a "book a demo" conversation with diagnostic centres
and hospitals. Scope: everything a designer and copywriter need to build the landing page.
Audience strategy, page structure section by section with example copy, a full color system
with hex codes, typography, layout and responsive rules, components, imagery, accessibility,
discoverability, and a handoff checklist.
House rules apply to every word that ships: no em dashes, no AI-sounding filler, no fabricated
statistics, and no price baked into the page that can go stale (see §9). Currency reference: 1
USD = 123 BDT.
The load-bearing honesty rule for this launch: we lead with the complete platform, but the
only thing shown as available today is the appointment module. Every other module is
"early access," "rolling out," or "talk to us," in honest words, never a finished-product claim and
never a faked screenshot (§9, §14). The platform is real and defined as an 18-module system;
most of it is still being built, so the page invites facilities into an early-access conversation
rather than claiming a finished hospital system.
How to read this brief: §1 to §3 are the strategy and the look. §4 to §8 are the build. §9 to §17
are the rules, the guardrails, and the handoff. A designer can start from §4. A copywriter can
start from §7.

2. Positioning: the complete platform, and how we stand apart
Lofi Care is not a chamber app. It is the system that runs the facility: an 18-module hospital
and diagnostic centre management platform covering patients, doctors and referrers, billing,
samples and reports, imaging, inpatient, pharmacy, workforce, and analytics. The page has to
make that breadth clear in the first screen, while being honest that appointments is what a
visitor can switch on today.
The category we enter is narrower than us. The visible local players are chamber and
prescription tools built for one doctor's desk. Lofi Care runs the whole institution. Say "one
platform for your whole facility," not "another app for your chamber."
We win the facility conversation on four things, not on being the biggest: 1. Built for
Bangladesh. Local workflows, local payment rails, Bangla and English, and the data stays in the
country (§7.10). Global enterprise systems are not built for a Chittagong diagnostic centre. 2.
One connected system. Every counter, lab, and department reads the same patient record. No
stitched-together tools. 3. Diagnostics depth that maps to how these centres actually make
money. Referrer commissions and earnings, barcoded samples from collection to delivery,
result verification and quality control, imaging, and fast report release. This is the sharpest
wedge; lead the diagnostic-centre pitch with it (§7.5). 4. You can start today, free, on the one
module that touches every patient: appointments. No six-month procurement to see value.
The differentiation line for the page: the complete hospital and diagnostic-centre platform
built for Bangladesh, that you can start using this week.

3. Audience strategy: three doors, one page
Three readers on a single page, each with a clear path.
Facility decision-maker (diagnostic centre or hospital owner or manager): the primary
target. Wants to know Lofi Care can run their operation and that it is credible. Path: the
platform and diagnostics story, then Book a demo. The facility door serves two sub-
audiences, diagnostic centres and hospitals, each with its own section (§7.5, §7.6).
Independent doctor: the self-serve entry. Wants their chamber to run on time. Path: the
appointment module, then Start free.
Patient: wants to book without a phone call. Path: Find your doctor.
Layout approach: a platform-first hero that speaks to the facility, with the doctor and patient
doors clearly visible right below it. The facility is the headline; the doctor and patient are
unmistakable but secondary. One page, three doors.
4. Color system
A calm, clinical, credible base with one warm color that does the asking. It has to carry both a
patient-friendly warmth and the institutional trust a hospital or diagnostic-centre owner needs.
Verify every text-on-background pair against WCAG AA (4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI)
before finalizing. Hex values are a starting point; keep the roles, tune to pass contrast.

4.2 Color rules
Marigold is a promise. Only on the single most important button in each section. One
primary CTA color per screen.
Never use red for a call to action. In healthcare, red reads as blood, emergency, and
alarm. Red is reserved for genuine errors.
Module status uses color honestly: Calm Green for "available now" (appointments
only), neutral Mist for "early access" or "on the roadmap." Never paint an unbuilt module
in the available color (§9).
White and Sky Wash carry the page. Saturated color is the exception, not the field.
Contrast is a hard requirement. Check Marigold's label color before shipping.

both free and web-served for speed.

desktop, so the desktop layout of the platform and diagnostics sections must look
credible and substantial.

("Book a demo") right; quiet links to the doctor and patient sections. Few exit paths.
Sticky mobile CTA bar: a bottom bar that always shows the primary action.
Container width: about 1140px max on desktop, centered, 16px side gutter on mobile,
no horizontal scroll.
Section rhythm: alternate White and Sky Wash. Generous vertical spacing (64 to 96px
desktop, 40 to 56px mobile).

Voice: plain, confident, credible. Short sentences.

11. Naming
Use consistently (§17). Recommended: Lofi Care is the platform and the brand; the live module
is Lofi Care Appointments; the facility product is the Lofi Care platform (or "Lofi Care for
Diagnostics and Hospitals"). Every module in §7.4 is named under the one Lofi Care platform.
Naming the module now, while only it is live, avoids a rebrand when the rest arrives.
12. Language strategy
English primary, Bangla available. The facility buyer and the younger doctor read
English comfortably, and English reads as modern and credible. A real EN / বাংলা  toggle,
not machine translation baked in.
What must be bilingual: the patient-facing section and booking flow, the primary CTAs,
and the trust and compliance lines.
Ship English with the patient path bilingual at minimum; full depth is a decision (§17).
13. Accessibility and performance
WCAG AA across the page: contrast (§4), visible focus states, real alt text, labelled form
fields, error messaging in text and not color alone.
Tap targets at least 44px, with spacing.
Performance is a conversion feature: fast first load on a mid-range Android on 4G,
compressed and lazy-loaded images, modern formats, two fonts only, no heavy above-
the-fold video.
Render the core message and CTA even if a large image is still loading.
14. What NOT to do
Never present a module that is not built as available, and never show a fabricated
screenshot of one (§9). First rule of this launch.
No fabricated stats or fake social proof. No invented user counts, no stock
testimonials, no fake client logos.
No no-show percentage stated as fact until measured.
No fixed platform price on the page, and no appointment price hard-coded in a way
that can rot.
No red call-to-action buttons.
No claim of HIPAA or SOC2 certification. We align with good practice and keep data
in Bangladesh; we do not certify.

hospital, diagnostic centre, and appointment keywords in the natural copy.
Favicon and app title set, so a saved link looks legitimate.
